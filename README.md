This commission is part of 'orbit,' a series of experimental websites, or "code-generated poems," about visibility. Each poem evokes the moon as a body pulled by a force beyond itself, at times illuminated, other times eclipsed. Such imagery is articulated through the relationship between front-end and back-end environments, which operate as an analogy for how marginalisation becomes legible in some contexts while constrained or obscured in others. By recontextualising familiar browser behaviours, the poems interrupt and foreground the programmed nature of interactions that otherwise feel instinctive, demonstrating how both interfaces and social norms are informed by choices hidden by design.

'an Apparition' takes the form of a fragmented poem: a sentence left unfinished, an identity mid-formation. A blinking cursor beckons as if an invitation to continue writing, to complete what's been left unsaid. But as visitors write about who they are, an auto-complete tool trained on gendered Reddit forums is already deciding for them.

The jump to a conclusion echoes something familiar: algorithmic feeds that profile and categorise us, influencing what we see and who we are encouraged to become. Large language models are not neutral. They learn from training data that sorts identity into reductive categories, conflating masculinity with dominance and rationality, and femininity with the emotional and passive. This sorting becomes visible within 'an Apparition,' where language read as feminine blurs, fades, falls, becoming harder to see with every accepted suggestion. The weight of bias accumulates gradually, until the consequences of erasure become undeniable.
